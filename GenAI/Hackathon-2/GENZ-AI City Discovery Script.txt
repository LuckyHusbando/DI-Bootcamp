# GENZ-AI | AI City Discovery

5-minute presentation script

Slide 1 — Title | 0:00–0:20

“We’re GENZ-AI, and this is AI City Discovery: a personalized place-recommendation system for Tel Aviv.

The idea is simple: less time scrolling through places, more time actually going to them.”

Slide 2 — Problem | 0:20–0:50

“Finding places is not the hard part. Finding the right place is.

A search for ‘quiet, creative, and affordable’ is more nuanced than a list of popular venues.

Existing discovery tools often give us options, but not enough context to decide.”

Slide 3 — Solution | 0:50–1:15

“Our app takes a natural-language request, identifies what matters, and returns a ranked shortlist with explanations.

Instead of forcing users to translate their plans into filters, they can simply describe what they want.”

Slide 4 — System flow | 1:15–2:00

“The pipeline has three AI stages.

First, DistilBERT with a LoRA adapter classifies preferences.

Second, Sentence Transformers creates semantic embeddings, and FAISS retrieves relevant places.

Third, a hybrid ranking stage orders the results, and Llama 3.2 through Ollama generates explanations.

If the local language model is unavailable, we have a fallback.”

Slide 5 — Recommendation engine | 2:00–2:35

“Ranking combines two main signals: 55 percent semantic similarity and 45 percent preference-tag coverage.

We also apply adjustments for evidence, data quality, price, and neighborhood.

So a place isn't recommended just because it contains the right keyword—or because it's popular.”

Slide 6 — LoRA training | 2:35–3:15

“The preference classifier uses DistilBERT, fine-tuned with LoRA across 22 preference categories.

Only 1.1147 percent of the model parameters were trainable.

Across three epochs, training loss fell from 0.4707 to 0.2771—about a 41 percent reduction.

Important caveat: that's training loss, not a held-out accuracy result. We still need independent validation.”

Slide 7 — Live demo | 3:15–4:25

“Let's try: ‘Find a quiet, affordable café with outdoor seating.’

The system extracts those preferences, retrieves semantically relevant places, ranks them, and explains the matches.

The point is not just to return a café. It's to show why each result fits the request.”

Demo actions:

1. Enter the prompt.
2. Show the extracted preferences.
3. Show the top recommendations.
4. Open one explanation.

Slide 8 — Limitations and conclusion | 4:25–5:00

“This is a working prototype, not a live city-wide data platform.

The demo dataset includes synthetic data. There is no live Google Places integration, persistent user profile, or feedback loop yet.

Next steps are verified live data, user feedback, and held-out model validation.

The goal is straightforward: better-fit recommendations with less searching.

Thank you.”

### Delivery notes

1. Keep the demo to approximately 70 seconds. If the app is slow, explain the pipeline and move on.
2. Don't claim the LoRA classifier is accurate yet. The supplied metric is training loss only.
3. Don't describe synthetic demo records as real venues.
4. If asked about evaluation, explain that the project includes a recommendation-evaluation framework, but no final metric values are being presented.

Timing: Aim for 4:40 in rehearsal. That leaves 20 seconds of breathing room.
